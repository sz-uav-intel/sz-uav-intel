One-network flight: cities are buying drone work as a service, from their own platforms

2026-11-16 · Shenzhen UAV & Low-Altitude Intelligence Briefing · Free edition

Executive summary. The clearest signal in this cycle's harvest is not a new policy but a purchasing pattern: municipal governments are moving from buying airframes to buying "one-network unified flight" (一网统飞) services — routine inspection and emergency response flown on demand — and the contracts are being won by their own low-altitude platform companies. Kunming's Panlong district awarded RMB 8.56 million for combined government-plus-police flight services, Huangshi's data bureau awarded RMB 5.65 million for "government low-altitude purchased services", Hami's development commission awarded RMB 4.91 million for a supervision platform, and Jingmen is tendering drone and payload packages under a 100% self-funded municipal infrastructure project. Upstream, the state has already set the operating frame — safety first, "cargo before passengers, isolated before integrated, outer suburbs before the urban core" — and downstream, private capital is rotating toward operators and airworthiness enablers rather than airframe programmes alone.

Service contracts for continuous drone patrol only make commercial sense on top of that coverage layer. [Source: Yicai, 2026-04-17, citing the MIIT document — link]

3 · Who wins: utilities and municipal platform companies

The counterpart of service-based buying is a service-based supplier, and in this harvest the suppliers are state vehicles with an airspace operating licence rather than drone manufacturers.

  • China Southern Power Grid's aviation arm outsources airspace operations. On 2026-11-03, CSG General Aviation Service Co. published the award for its 2026–2027 first batch of low-altitude airspace operating services; the winner is Guangdong Low-Altitude Security Technology Co., with no contract value disclosed in the notice. A grid utility buying airspace operations as a standing service — not a one-off equipment order — is the clearest sign yet that power-line patrol has become a recurring flight programme rather than a project. [Source: China Southern Power Grid supply-chain platform, 2026-11-03 — link]
  • The same pattern repeats at municipal level. Huangshi Quanyu Low-Altitude Economy Development Co. took the data bureau's RMB 5.65 million service contract; Jingmen Low-Altitude Industry Investment & Development Co. is procuring flight equipment for its own network. [Sources: 十环招标网, 2026-11-06; Hubei public-resources platform, 2026-11-07 — linked in Section 2]

For foreign suppliers the practical consequence is a channel question rather than a price question: the entity that decides which airframe and payload fly the patrol is increasingly a local platform company or a utility subsidiary, and the tender is written for the service, not the product. [Inference — our view, based on the awards above]

4 · Capital: toward operators and airworthiness enablers

  • Zero Boundary Aviation (零界航空) raises a near-RMB 10 million angel round to run other people's aircraft. The Anhui-based eVTOL trading and capacity-services company closed a round of just under RMB 10 million from Shanghai Huxiang Investment as sole investor, reported 2026-11-10, to build and operate a validation base, complete its OC operating system, widen its OEM network and build a digital platform. It does not build aircraft: in September 2026 it signed a three-party strategic agreement with Fengfei Aviation (峰飞航空) and Wanjiang Financial Leasing covering the purchase of 30 V2000CG aircraft, with the first five due within the year and two already delivered in September; it runs a regional operating platform with Fengfei in Binzhou, Shandong, and a leasing-based project in Yulin, Shaanxi with a local state investment platform intended to validate asset pricing and end-of-term buy-back. [Source: Zhitong Caijing, 2026-11-10 — link]
  • Airworthiness tooling attracts repeat rounds. Shenzhen Qingwei Aviation completed a round of more than RMB 50 million on 2026-10-20 led by CITIC Construction Investment Capital, Optics Valley Sci-Tech Investment and Jiangxi Financial Holding Capital — its third round in six months, after Hillhouse Ventures, Songhe Capital, Plum Ventures and Guotai Junan Ventures — to accelerate a DAL-A fly-by-wire computing platform and smart cockpit displays through airworthiness. [Source: Yuanxiang Low-Altitude Industry Observer, 2026-10-26 — link]

The two cases point the same way as this spring's airworthiness-heavy funding: once type certification separates "can fly" from "can carry passengers for money", capital is priced on operating capability and certification support, not on airframe count. [Inference — our view, based on the two sources above]

5 · Industrial geography: a Corps cluster in the west, a test range inland

  • Shihezi (Xinjiang): the Production and Construction Corps is building a cluster, not a demo. The third Low-Altitude Industry Innovation Conference was held in Shihezi (reported 2026-11-09), with seven cooperation agreements signed, including an intelligent unmanned equipment and swarm test base and a low-altitude meteorology agreement. The Corps reports the second production line at Tianyu Hangtong's Shihezi manufacturing base now in operation, Shihezi Aviation Vocational College through Ministry of Education filing, a plateau-UAV academician workstation running, the HY100 large UAV passing 10 million mu of cumulative treated area, and the country's first cross-Bohai large-UAV commercial logistics route opened. [Source: Science and Technology Daily (stdaily.com), 2026-11-09 — link]
  • Xiangcheng county (Xuchang, Henan): a county buys a scenario-validation facility. Reported 2026-11-12, Henan Xiangyi General Aviation signed a tripartite agreement with the China Academy of Industrial Internet's network institute and Beijing Qingyan Gonglian Aviation Technology on low-altitude data-intelligent infrastructure — a low-altitude big-data centre, secure communications modules, a low-altitude data operating system with a collaborative workbench and agents, and a parts-traceability platform — and separately a framework agreement with Tongzhi Data Technology (Beijing) for a low-altitude scenario-validation facility: about 35 mu in the county vocational-education park, roughly RMB 40 million total investment, comprising a flight digital-model collaboration centre, scenario-validation grounds and a low-altitude intelligent-manufacturing platform, serving high-voltage line, rail, tunnel, fire, solar and chemical-pipeline inspection, plus algorithm testing, manipulator training and inspection datasets. [Source: xuchang.gov.cn, 2026-11-12 — link]

Test infrastructure is moving to where the industrial customers are — a county with high-voltage lines and chemical pipelines rather than an aviation hub. That is a demand-led build-out, distinct from the exhibition-centre wave of 2024–25. [Inference — our view, based on the xuchang.gov.cn source above]

The takeaway

The three dated hooks for foreign readers this cycle are the procurement unit change — districts, data bureaus and DRCs buying flight-as-a-service at RMB 5–9 million a contract — the supplier concentration in municipal platform companies and utility subsidiaries (CSG's airspace-operations award to Guangdong Low-Altitude Security Technology), and the approval-efficiency promise, with the NDRC explicitly studying local "scan-to-fly" schemes. If those approvals compress as promised, the service contracts already let on city balance sheets are the revenue line that scales; if they do not, the same contracts become a subsidy by another name. That is the variable to watch in the next two quarters, not the number of new industrial parks. [Inference — our view, based on the sources cited above]
