The plan period begins: standards first, and a much bigger fleet to regulate

2026-11-09 · Shenzhen UAV & Low-Altitude Intelligence Briefing · Free edition

Executive summary. This week's harvest reads like the opening of the 15th Five-Year plan period: the CPC Central Committee's Recommendations for the 2026–2030 plan (published October 2025) put the low-altitude economy inside the national planning frame, and the CAAC administrator has since confirmed that the final Outline mandates "promoting the healthy and orderly development" of the sector. In parallel, ten departments led by the market regulator have issued a Low-Altitude Economy Standards System Construction Guide targeting 300+ standards by 2030 — a compliance grid foreign component and systems suppliers will need to map. The fleet those rules must absorb has grown fast: the official count of registered drones reached 3.287 million at end-2025, up from 1.267 million at end-2023, while eVTOL maker Vertaxi closed a near-RMB 1 billion round led by state insurance capital and two listed industrial-drone makers paired booming revenue with negative operating cash flow.

2 · What the pilots already deliver: Hunan's numbers

Against the plan-level news above, one primary document shows what a mature airspace-reform pilot actually produces. Hunan's free-trade-zone office recounts the province's national-first low-altitude reform package:

  • The country's first provincial low-altitude airspace designation plan: 97 visual flight routes, enabling time-shared, layered sharing of airspace between general aviation and the military and civil aviation authorities.
  • Surveillance built on a "BeiDou + ADS-B + 5G" tri-mode network of 71 ground stations, plus the first provincial low-altitude aeronautical chart, achieving province-wide low-altitude surveillance coverage.
  • Process compression as the headline result: flight-plan filing cut from 9 steps to 3, and approval time from 3–5 days to 2 hours; by end-2024 the Changsha FTZ low-altitude area had added 69 new general-aviation companies.
    • [Source: ftz.hunan.gov.cn (Hunan Free Trade Zone office), 2025-07-04 — link]

"Approval time from days to hours" is the metric most worth watching as other provinces replicate the model in the plan period — it is the operational definition of whether low-altitude logistics and inspection can run at commercial cadence. [Inference — our view, based on the source above]

3 · The new official baseline: 3.287 million registered drones

Issue 003 carried the end-2023 official baseline (1.267 million registered drones). This week's harvest updates the yardstick: a state-media policy commentary citing the CAAC statistical bulletin reports that by end-2025, 3.287 million drones were registered nationwide — roughly 2.6× the end-2023 figure in two years. The same commentary notes China's deepening role in ISO/IEC low-altitude standardization, including co-leading the UAS Detect-and-Avoid Systems international standard, and the 2026 Low-Altitude Economy Industry Development Conference in Zigong with 300+ participating companies. [Source: ce.cn (Economic Daily), 2026-11-04 — link]

The doubling of the registered fleet between the two official baselines is our arithmetic on the two regulator-cited figures; the 2030 standards programme in Section 1 is the state's answer to governing it. [Inference — our view, based on the gov.cn and ce.cn sources above]

The takeaway

The 15th Five-Year period has started the way mature industries do: with a plan, a standards grid and a police code, rather than with new subsidies. The three dated hooks for foreign readers this week are the 2030 standards deadline (300+ standards, ten departments), the end-2025 fleet baseline of 3.287 million registered drones that the grid must govern, and the 1 January 2026 entry into force of Shandong's drone public-security measures as the model for provincial enforcement. Between them, procurement awards show counties and state infrastructure groups buying complete low-altitude stacks, and capital keeps rotating toward players with orders and airworthiness capability rather than airframes alone.
