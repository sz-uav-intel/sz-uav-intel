Issue 003 · Weekly Edition · Policy deep-dive

The 2027 insurance mandate, and the scaffolding beneath it

2026-11-02 · Shenzhen UAV & Low-Altitude Intelligence Briefing · Free edition

Executive summary. This week's monitoring surfaced an unusually clean read on Beijing's regulatory intent, because the harvest is dominated by primary documents. The most consequential is the February 2026 tri-agency Implementation Opinion on Low-Altitude Insurance, whose full text sets a hard milestone: by 2027, a mandatory liability-insurance regime for unmanned aircraft is to be "initially established" — a date foreign insurers, lessors and fleet operators can now plan against. Behind it sits a decade of scaffolding that is easy to miss from outside: a three-tier flight-service system piloted since 2018, and an official end-2023 baseline (1.267 million registered drones, 690 general-aviation companies, 449 GA airports) against which all subsequent policy targets are measured.

2 · The official baseline: what Beijing counts

Regulator-published statistics are the cleanest public yardstick for market size claims. At a CAAC press briefing (as republished by gov.cn), the transport department's deputy director Shang Kejia gave the end-2023 baseline:

  • 690 registered general-aviation companies — 2.5× the 2015 level.
  • 2,900 aircraft in operation (1.5× 2015); average monthly flight volume 114,000 hours (1.8× 2015).
  • 1.267 million registered drones, up 32.2% year-on-year, operated by ~19,000 companies.
  • 449 general-aviation airports — 7.4× the 2015 level.
  • Safety: 2023 GA flight volume +27.5% vs 2019, while the accident rate per 10,000 sorties fell 42.1% vs 2019.
    • [Source: gov.cn policy interpretation "低空经济'振翅高飞'", 2024-04-01 — link]

These are 2023 figures — the most recent full official baseline in this harvest — and every later policy target (the 2027 insurance regime, provincial flight-service networks) is built on top of them. [Inference — our view, based on the sources above]

3 · From pilot to system: the flight-service backbone

A 2024 signed article by CAAC chief Song Zhiyong sets out how today's infrastructure regime was assembled — useful context for any entrant wondering who builds and operates the ground layer:

  • 2010: State Council & Central Military Commission issue the Opinion on Deepening Low-Altitude Airspace Management Reform; CAAC follows with guidance for building and managing general-aviation flight service stations.
  • 2018: the national air traffic control committee approves Sichuan as the comprehensive low-altitude airspace reform pilot, producing a model led by local governments with military–local–civil joint participation.
  • Since 2020: the pilot expands to Hunan, Jiangxi and Anhui, under CAAC's Overall Plan for Building the Low-Altitude Flight Service Support System, which defines a national / regional / flight-service-station three-tier structure.
    • [Source: caac.gov.cn, "高质量建设低空飞行服务保障体系" by CAAC administrator Song Zhiyong, 2024-04-09 — link]

Complementing this, a CAAC report published ahead of the 2024 "two sessions" records the policy-timeline milestones: the 2021 National Comprehensive Three-Dimensional Transport Network Plan was the first national plan to include the low-altitude economy; the December 2023 Central Economic Work Conference named it a strategic emerging industry to build; the Interim Regulations on UAV Flight Management took effect 1 January 2024; and the February 2024 Central Financial & Economic Affairs Commission meeting tied low-altitude logistics into national logistics-cost policy, with the 2024 government work report calling it a new growth engine. [Source: caacnews.com.cn (CAAC newspaper), March 2024 per document URL — link]

4 · Global demand signals

  • The 2027 mandatory UAS liability-insurance milestone is the single most actionable datum this week for foreign readers: re/insurers, fleet lessors and financing structures serving China-linked UAS operations now have an official date around which to price cover, and Chinese insurers will be racing to build product shelves before it. [Inference — our view, based on the 2026-02-12 tri-agency Opinion]
  • The three-tier flight-service architecture and the province-level pilots (Sichuan → Hunan, Jiangxi, Anhui) explain the procurement wave Issue 002 documented at city level (Mianyang's vertiport planning, Zibo's landing-point EPC): flight-service stations and their ground systems are the designated budget lines. [Inference — our view, based on the 2024-04-09 CAAC article and Issue 002 procurement listings]

The takeaway

When the search pipes narrow, read what the state itself publishes. This week's documents trace a twelve-year arc — airspace-reform opinion (2010) → Sichuan pilot (2018) → three-tier service system → UAS flight regulations (2024) → insurance mandate (2026, effective milestone 2027). The direction of travel is consistent: every layer of the low-altitude economy is being converted from pilot programmes into permanent, insurable, budgeted institutions. For market entrants, the 2027 insurance deadline and the flight-service-station build-out are the two dated, money-backed hooks.
