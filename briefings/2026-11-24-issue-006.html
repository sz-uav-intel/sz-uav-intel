Build-out, not aircraft: carriers, contractors and county platforms are absorbing the low-altitude budget

2026-11-24 · Shenzhen UAV & Low-Altitude Intelligence Briefing · Free edition

Executive summary. This cycle's harvest is dominated by construction and integration contracts rather than aircraft: a Jinan programme approved at RMB 703.65 million, a RMB 19.53 million county-level "one-network unified flight" (一网统飞) project in Wuxue, and three telecom-carrier tenders in Shandong, Hubei and Jilin for drone fleets and flight-service platforms. The qualification rules say plainly who is expected to do the work — municipal engineering design and construction grades in Jinan, software R&D capability in Wuxue — while Jilin's new provincial subsidy rules explicitly exclude general airports, large air-traffic facilities and government investment projects, which pushes the concrete onto state-owned balance sheets instead of fiscal budgets. For suppliers outside China the practical consequence is that the airframe and payload decision is increasingly buried inside an infrastructure or IT package, and the party writing the specification is a contractor, a carrier or a county platform company rather than an aviation buyer.

1 · Policy: a provincial subsidy that is deliberately not for concrete

Jilin's finance department and development and reform commission issued Measures for the Management of the Special Fund for High-Quality Low-Altitude Economy Development (吉财产业〔2026〕445号, dated 2026-06-25, published 2026-09-07). The fund comes from the provincial budget and is restricted to two targets: projects on the provincial low-altitude application-scenario list and pilot and demonstration cities. Allocation is by project, decided by the provincial DRC, and paid as a post-hoc reward (事后奖补) — money follows a completed result, not a promise.

The eligibility text is the interesting part. Supported costs include communication, navigation, surveillance and counter-UAS equipment, the operation and equipment upgrade of aircraft in agriculture, logistics, passenger transport, tourism, medical and urban-management scenarios, and flight-operation insurance premiums. Explicitly not supported: general-aviation airports and large air-traffic-control facilities, and government investment projects. The fund may not cover staff or running costs, buildings, fines, donations, investments or debt service, and may not double-fund a project already supported elsewhere. It runs to 2028-12-31, with application notices due by the end of June for the following year.

[Source: Jilin Provincial Government information disclosure portal (xxgk.jl.gov.cn), 2026-09-07 — link; the same document is published by the provincial finance department (czt.jl.gov.cn) — grade A]

Read against the procurement below, the rule set is coherent rather than restrictive: provincial fiscal money is steered into operating low-altitude activity — flying, insuring, equipping — while building the low-altitude layer is left to state-owned enterprises funding it off their own balance sheets. That division of labour is the quiet structural fact of this cycle. [Inference — our view, based on the Jilin measures and the Jinan and Wuxue funding structures cited below]

2 · Contractors: the vertiport EPC asks for municipal engineering, not aviation

Jinan's digital low-altitude flight-management platform infrastructure programme is the largest single commitment in this harvest. Its approved total investment is RMB 703.65 million (project approval 2411-370100-04-01-419494), the owner is Jinan Low-Altitude Economy Development Group Co., and the funding is 100% state-owned "non-fiscal" investment — that is, not a government budget line.

For a foreign reader the consequence is concrete — the specification for what lands on those pads will be written by municipal design institutes, and any imported equipment has to fit a package defined by Chinese engineering practice. [Inference — our view, based on the two Jinan notices above]

3 · Counties: one contract for nests, cloud, platform, pilots and maintenance

Wuxue, a county-level city in Hubei, tendered phase one of its low-altitude economy construction project on 2026-11-09 with a contract estimate of RMB 19.53 million, a 365-day build, a planned start of 2026-12-12 and a bid deadline of 2026-12-02. The owner is Wuxue Lingang Technology Co., a port-industry state enterprise, and the funding is 100% self-raised. The notice states the project is not a government procurement works project.

What is being bought is a complete operating layer, not equipment: 20 fixed-point intelligent drone nests, 2 vehicle-mounted inspection platforms, 1 high-precision surveying drone and 2 heavy-lift firefighting drones, plus network transmission and cross-domain security boundaries (drone-side, nest-side, ground relay, command centre), leased fixed and mobile links, leased government-cloud compute, storage, security and hosting, a "one-network unified flight" management platform with inspection management, a flight command centre, a business middle platform, a flight control platform, a data management platform and a flight-service app with cross-platform interfaces, and finally maintenance, pilot and operations staff stationed on site plus emergency support. Applicant qualification: legal-person status and software R&D or information-system construction capability.

[Source: Hubei electronic bidding platform (bidcloud.hbggzy.cn), 2026-11-09 — link; grade A]

This is the pattern worth tracking: a county with no aviation industry buys the whole stack — hardware, cloud, software, pilots, five-year-style operations — under one software qualification, on its own balance sheet. It means the drone content is a sub-line of an IT and operations contract, and the integrator, not the buyer, chooses the airframes. [Inference — our view, based on the Wuxue notice]

4 · Carriers: three telecom operators are wiring up city networks

Three separate procurement notices in three provinces put China's telecom operators on the buyer side of low-altitude infrastructure rather than the connectivity side only.

  • China Mobile, Shandong. A 2026 tender (No. SDYD20260500675, published 2026-10-01) for a low-altitude economy flight-service management platform and AI drone hardware, tendered by China Mobile Shandong Co. with self-raised funds and 公诚管理咨询 as agent. A carrier is buying both the platform layer and the aircraft. [Source: Shandong tender aggregator (shandong.jianyu360.cn), 2026-10-01 — link; grade C]
  • China Unicom, Shiyan (Hubei). A comparative-selection notice (2026-11-02) for the Shiyan low-altitude intelligent networked system: 12 integrated drone-and-automated-nest sets plus five years of operations and maintenance, budget RMB 4.0 million excluding VAT, ceiling RMB 3.42 million, commissioning within 90 days of signature. The five-year O&M term is the tell — a carrier is taking on standing operational responsibility, not a one-off purchase. [Source: bid aggregator (yfbzb.com), 2026-11-02 — link; grade C]
  • China Telecom, Liaoyuan (Jilin). A comparative-selection notice (2026-11-11, bid deadline 2026-11-19) for low-altitude economy drones, estimated at RMB 149,000. The stated rationale is that ground patrol coverage is limited and inefficient, and that equipment is needed "to land the one-network-unified-flight project" — a municipal-level carrier branch buying aircraft to support a city flight programme. [Source: bid aggregator (qianlima.com), 2026-11-11 — link; grade C]

The three notices are small in value and all three are known only through tender aggregators, so treat the individual figures as indicative. The pattern, however, is consistent with the city-level contracts in sections 2 and 3: carriers hold the network, the cloud and the integration teams, which makes them the natural prime contractor for a municipal drone network — and puts them in competition with the municipal platform companies that won last cycle's service contracts. [Inference — our view, based on the three aggregator notices above]

6 · Pipeline: 86 projects in Chongqing, a test base in Hebei

  • Chongqing publishes an 86-project opportunity list worth about RMB 28.9 billion. At a low-altitude governance and talent conference on 2026-11-21 the municipality released an 86-item low-altitude industry opportunity list with estimated total investment of about RMB 28.9 billion, across five categories — infrastructure, equipment manufacturing, research and talent education, operation services, and testing and operational support — including landing facilities, communication-navigation-surveillance, digital base layers, complete-aircraft R&D, key components and intelligent payloads. Eight projects were signed on the day at about RMB 1.79 billion, covering industrial investment, school-local cooperation, platform building, application scenarios and industry-academia-research links. [Source: China News Service (chinanews.com.cn), 2026-11-21 — link; grade B]
  • Handan's Cheng'an county lands a tethered-drone test and repair base. Reported 2026-10-26: a domestic leader in tethered drones (系留无人机) signed to establish a flight-test and repair base in Cheng'an county, Hebei, initially leasing 5,000 m² of plant for dynamic testing and equipment servicing, with later plans for a heavy-lift industrial UAV production base, an exhibition and operations centre and a training base. The county's pitch is plain airspace, precision-machining suppliers and its own "one-network unified flight" governance platform. [Source: Hebei Radio and Television (hebtv.com), 2026-10-26 — link; grade B]

An opportunity list is a pipeline, not revenue: the RMB 28.9 billion is an estimate of project value published by the municipality, and only the RMB 1.79 billion of signed agreements has moved past intention. What matters for suppliers is the category mix — infrastructure and testing sit above aircraft manufacturing, which is the same weighting visible in the contracts above. [Inference — our view, based on the Chinanews report]

The takeaway

Three dated hooks for foreign readers this cycle. One: the money is being spent on the layer beneath the aircraft — Jinan's RMB 703.65 million programme is civil works with a digital overlay, and its EPC packages are gated on municipal engineering qualifications, with the flagship hub awarded to an energy contractor and a city design institute. Two: the integrator is changing — telecom carriers in Shandong, Hubei and Jilin are now buying platforms, drone fleets and five-year O&M, competing with the municipal platform companies that took last cycle's service contracts. Three: the fiscal rules are explicit that infrastructure is not a subsidy item (Jilin), so build-out is financed off state balance sheets (Jinan "non-fiscal", Wuxue "self-raised"), which makes the pace of construction a function of local SOE credit rather than of policy enthusiasm. If that credit tightens, the aircraft orders embedded in these packages are the first item to be deferred. [Inference — our view, based on the sources cited above]
