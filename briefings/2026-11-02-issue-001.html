State of the Shenzhen Cluster: the baseline every drone market entrant should have

2026-11-02 · Shenzhen UAV & Low-Altitude Intelligence Briefing · Free edition

Why this issue. As our first briefing, we fix the baseline: verified, sourced figures on the Shenzhen cluster — where roughly 70% of the world's consumer drones and about half of its industrial drones are made — plus the compliance agenda that will shape who can sell what, where, in 2027. Subsequent issues return to weekly cadence across our four pillars.

Watch item

Meituan's 4th-generation drone received China's first nationwide low-altitude logistics operation certificate — a regulatory precedent that lowers the path for other delivery operators. [Source: Shenzhen SAR Government Online, Feb 2026]

2 · Policy & Certification Tracker

  • Shenzhen's Special Economic Zone Low-Altitude Economy Industry Promotion Regulations — China's first local legislation dedicated to the low-altitude economy — took effect in 2024 and anchors a full policy toolkit: eVTOL airworthiness subsidies, route incentives, and a RMB 1 bn low-altitude industry guidance fund. [Source: People's Daily Shenzhen, Feb 2026]
  • At national level, the 2026 World Drone Conference was told China's low-altitude economy reached RMB 1.5 trillion in market size for 2025. [Source: China News Service, Mar 2026]
  • Provincial race: Guangdong's action plan targets a RMB 3 trillion-plus low-altitude economy by 2026 across the Guangzhou–Shenzhen–Zhuhai triangle; Beijing and Jiangsu have issued their own implementation plans. Provincial competition for pilots, airspace and funds is now structural. [Sources: Guangdong Provincial Government, May 2024; Beijing MIIT Bureau, Sep 2024; Jiangsu Provincial Government, Sep 2024]

3 · Export Compliance Watch — the season is open

  • Announcement No. 49 (drone export): on 27 Oct 2026 the Shenzhen UAV Industry Association convened a feedback session with China Customs Science & Technology Research Center, Shenzhen Customs and the China Airborne Research Academy — implementation guidance is still moving, and exporters' pain points are being collected. [Source: SZUAVIA, Oct 2026]
  • Brazil: ANAC certification was reported as a recurring blocker for drone exporters; a dedicated Shenzhen seminar with Brazilian certification experts ran in August 2026. [Source: SZUAVIA, Aug 2026]
  • Why it matters: for foreign buyers, "can the supplier ship legally and certify locally?" is now the first question — ahead of price. Expect compliance capability to become a procurement criterion in Gulf, Latin American and EU tenders through 2027. [Inference — our view]

4 · Global Demand Signals

  • The World Drone Conference (Shenzhen, 21–23 May 2026, 11th edition) drew participants from 140+ countries and territories, 1,200+ exhibitors across 110,000 m², with 10,000+ targeted overseas buyers; overseas sessions were set in South Korea, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan — the practical shortlist for next quarter's demand mapping. [Sources: SZUAVIA; China News Service, Mar 2026]
  • Association reporting flagged Africa as a quality export market (Sep 2026) and hosted a UAE science & technology delegation (Oct 2026) — Gulf and African institutional demand continues to build. [Source: SZUAVIA]

The takeaway

The Shenzhen cluster is not slowing down — but the easy part (making drones) is finished business. The next competitive battleground is compliance and market access: whoever answers "can it be shipped, certified and insured in my country?" fastest wins the tender. That is where this briefing will focus week by week.
