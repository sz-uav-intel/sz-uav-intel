Policy locks in, budgets follow: from the five-year plan to vertiports

2026-11-02 · Shenzhen UAV & Low-Altitude Intelligence Briefing · Free edition

Executive summary. China's low-altitude economy is being embedded in permanent national architecture: the 15th Five-Year Plan outline (published March 2026) sets the 2026–2030 baseline, and a February 2026 tri-agency opinion extends that architecture into low-altitude insurance. At ground level, prefecture-level cities are now spending real money on airspace planning and landing infrastructure — Mianyang tendered a RMB 5 million planning contract for 20 vertiports, and Zibo put a phase-one EPC for 30 smart landing points to tender. Capital and earnings confirm a bifurcating market: H1 2026 primary-market funding reached roughly RMB 29.3 billion across 166 deals (concentrated in a handful of airframers), while listed industrial-drone leaders posted strong 2025 profits on persistently negative operating cash flow.

1 · Policy & Regulatory Developments

2026-03-13State Council (gov.cn) The full Outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan for National Economic and Social Development (2026–2030) was published — the top-level planning document to which all sector policies (CAAC rulemaking, NDRC programmes, provincial plans) now align. [Source: gov.cn, 2026-03-13 — link]

It defines low-altitude insurance to cover aircraft manufacturing and operations, low-altitude infrastructure, and specialised services — i.e. insurance is now treated as part of the safety-management backbone of the sector, not a side product. [Source: CAAC website, publication date Feb 2026 per document URL — link]
2025-09-19CAAC (via gov.cn) Two normative documents issued: the Interim Measures for the Air Sightseeing Market (29 articles — market-access conditions, service standards, pricing, consumer protection, supervision) and the Interim Measures for the Skydiving Flight Service Market (20 articles — definitions, access, safety and consumer-protection requirements). [Source: gov.cn, 2025-09-19 — link]
2025-07-04CAAC CAAC merged its three previous leading groups (general aviation; unmanned aircraft; low-altitude economy promotion) into a single General Aviation and Low-Altitude Economy Leading Group, chaired by the CAAC chief, with six specialised working groups covering planning, airworthiness certification, market supervision, operations oversight, flight-service platform construction and safety oversight. [Source: caac.gov.cn, 2025-07-04 — link]
2025-10-13Guangdong Provincial Government Several Measures for Supporting High-Quality Development of the Low-Altitude Economy issued, implementing the 2024–2026 action plan. Highlights: dynamic optimisation of the provincial low-altitude route network; a provincial comprehensive flight-service platform plus flight service stations in Guangzhou, Shenzhen and Zhuhai; and integration of drone safety/emergency management into local emergency-response systems — with responsibilities explicitly assigned to the Guangzhou, Shenzhen and Zhuhai governments. [Source: gd.gov.cn, 2025-10-13 — link]

Why it matters. For foreign market entrants, policy-driven demand (airspace planning, flight-service stations, landing infrastructure, insurance) is now on a 2026–2030 funding horizon rather than a pilot-program horizon. [Inference — our view, based on the documents above]

3 · Company & Industry Moves

  • Qingwei Aviation (Shenzhen) — RMB 50M+ round, third in six months. Shenzhen-based Qingwei Aviation (founded 2023; fly-by-wire flight-control computing platforms and smart-cockpit display systems for general aviation and advanced air mobility) raised over RMB 50 million from CITIC Construction Investment Capital, Optics Valley Sci-Tech Innovation Investment and Jiangxi Financial Holdings Capital, following earlier rounds from Hillhouse Ventures, Songhe Capital and Plum Ventures. Proceeds target DAL-A flight-control platform development and airworthiness progress. [Source: 10jqka.com.cn relaying Touzijiie (Investment Circle), 2026-10-20 — link]
  • H1 2026 funding: RMB ~29.3bn across 166 deals. Primary-market financing in the low-altitude economy reached RMB 29.289 billion over 166 deals in H1 2026 (per ITJuzi data as reported), already exceeding full-year 2025; ~25 large disclosed deals accounted for ~RMB 22.5 billion, with roughly 80% concentrated in five airframe makers. The same report notes Archer's US$850m June-2025 raise as the largest globally, faltering European players, and growing Middle-East capital inflows. [Source: 36Kr, 2026-08-06 — link]
  • Listed industrial-drone leaders: strong P&L, weak cash flow. 2025 annual reports show AVIC UAS (SH688297) with revenue above RMB 3 billion and net profit up more than twofold, and Zongheng Co. (SH688070) returning to profit; both concentrated earnings growth in Q4 2025 while operating cash flow stayed in net outflow. Reported market caps at reporting time: ~RMB 33.6 bn (AVIC UAS) and ~RMB 4.1 bn (Zongheng). [Source: National Business Daily (nbd.com.cn), 2026-04-01 — link]

4 · Global Demand Signals

  • Provincial procurement is becoming a demand signal for foreign component and systems suppliers: Mianyang's RMB 5M planning contract and Zibo's 30-point smart landing network imply follow-on equipment, communication and surveillance orders once designs are fixed. [Inference — our view]
  • The insurance framework (Section 1) matters for export markets: third-party liability and hull cover for UAS operations is a prerequisite in most non-Chinese tenders; China now building a domestic low-altitude insurance framework reduces a structural gap that previously pushed operators toward foreign (re)insurers. [Inference — our view, based on the Feb-2026 tri-agency opinion]

The takeaway

The policy stack is complete at every level — five-year plan, regulator machinery, market rules, insurance — and money is now moving down the stack into city-level construction contracts. Watch the procurement pipelines of second-tier cities (Mianyang, Zibo and peers): that is where equipment demand crystallises first, and where overseas suppliers can still enter before specifications harden.
